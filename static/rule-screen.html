Proposed § 40.11 Contract Screen

On June 10, 2026, the CFTC proposed amendments to Regulation 40.11 and a new Appendix F implementing the Commodity Exchange Act's Special Rule for event contracts (CEA § 5c(c)(5)(C)): Prediction Markets; Public Interest Determinations, RIN 3038-AF65. The proposal applies a three-step inquiry — scope, enumerated activity, public interest — on a contract-by-contract basis. This tool walks a contract design through the proposed framework as written, and connects the proposal's hedging-utility factor to the liquidity-constrained risk-transfer analysis developed in the paper.

This is an educational screen of a proposed rule — quotations are from the Commission-approved pre-publication version (June 9, 2026), which is subject to technical corrections and to change through the comment process. Outputs are a structured reading of the proposal's factors, not legal advice and not a prediction of any Commission determination. Consult counsel before relying on any output. Outputs depend entirely on the user's selected inputs and assumptions and are illustrative; they are not a determination, endorsement, or screening of any particular product, platform, or listing. This screen addresses only the proposed § 40.11 enumerated-activity / gaming analysis; contracts referencing price-based measures — financial, commodity, or digital-asset prices — fall outside that analysis and are screened out at Step 1.
Presets:

Step 1 — Scope

The Special Rule reaches event contracts in excluded commodities based on an occurrence, extent of an occurrence, or contingency (other than a price-based measure). The proposal also lists categories the Commission preliminarily views as generally outside the Special Rule.

Safe-list categories per the proposal's illustrative out-of-scope examples (economic indicators, financial indicators, FX, commodity and digital-asset reference prices and other price-based measures, election results and political activities, awards contests). Out-of-scope contracts remain subject to the generally applicable listing standards, including that contracts not be readily susceptible to manipulation.

Step 2 — Enumerated Activity

A contract "involves" an activity if its settlement is determined by an occurrence, extent of an occurrence, or contingency in the activity (proposed § 40.11(a)(3)). The inquiry looks at the underlying activity — the game on the field — not the act of trading the contract.

Step 3 — Public Interest Factors

If a contract involves an enumerated activity, the Commission weighs general factors (proposed § 40.11(a)(5)) and activity-specific factors (proposed § 40.11(a)(6)) on balance; no single factor is dispositive.

The proposal states that demonstrated hedging is not required, but that a "reasonable potential for a hedging or pricing function would be a significant factor against" an adverse finding, assessed by whether contracts "can meaningfully facilitate risk transfer and price discovery." The model below applies the paper's liquidity-constrained transfer analysis: hedge capacity is a joint function of the hedger's concentrated liability and market depth, so the inquiry should be run at reasonably foreseeable mature depth, not only at listing-day depth.
